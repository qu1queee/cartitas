# The Moon — middle (8–11)

<!-- age: middle | topic: space | subtopic: moon -->

Q: Why does the Moon shine if it is not a star?
A: The Moon does not make its own light. Sunlight hits the Moon and bounces toward Earth, so we see a bright circle or slice in the night sky.

---

Q: How far is the Moon from Earth?
A: The Moon is about 384,000 kilometers away. That is far, but it is still Earth's closest neighbor in space. A spacecraft usually takes about three days to get there.

---

Q: Why does the Moon have so many holes called craters?
A: Space rocks have smashed into the Moon for a very long time. Earth has rain, wind, and plants that wear craters away, but the Moon has almost no air or water, so the scars stay.

---

Q: Why do ocean tides rise and fall?
A: The Moon's gravity pulls on Earth's water and makes it bulge. As Earth turns, that bulge moves, so sea level goes up and down each day. The Sun pulls too, but the Moon is the main cause.
