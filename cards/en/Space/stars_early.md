# Stars — early (5–7)

<!-- age: early | lang: en | topic: space | subtopic: stars -->

Q: When can you see stars in the sky?
A: On clear nights when the sky is dark. The bright Sun hides them during the day.

---

C: Stars look like tiny bright [dots] in the night sky.

---

Q: Why do stars seem to twinkle?
A: Starlight bends a little as it passes through the air around Earth. That makes them sparkle.

---

Q: What is a constellation?
A: A constellation is a group of stars that makes a picture in the sky. One famous one is the Big Dipper.

---

Q: Are stars close to Earth?
A: Most stars are very, very far away. They look small because they are so far from us.

# Stars — early (5–7)

<!-- age: early | lang: en | topic: space | subtopic: stars -->

C: At [night], when the sky is dark, you can look up and spot many stars.

---

Q: What colors can stars look like?
A: Many look white or yellow to our eyes. Some look reddish. They can seem different colors because they are different temperatures.

---

Q: Do stars make sounds we can hear from Earth?
A: No. Space has almost no air to carry sound. Stars are silent—even the Sun would not make a noise we could hear from here.

---

Q: Why can clouds make it hard to see stars?
A: Clouds block your view like a gray blanket over the sky. When the sky is clear, many more stars can shine through.

---

Q: What is stargazing?
A: Looking up at the night sky to enjoy the stars. You can use just your eyes or simple binoculars on a clear, dark night.
