# Olympics — middle (8–11)

<!-- age: middle | lang: en | topic: sports | subtopic: olympics -->

Q: How often are the Summer Olympics held?
A: Every four years. Each time a different city hosts the games and invites athletes from around the world.

---

Q: What do the five Olympic rings stand for?
A: They stand for five parts of the world coming together in sport. The rings are linked to show friendship.

---

C: Athletes from many countries compete in the [Olympic Games].

---

Q: What is the difference between the Summer and Winter Games?
A: Summer Games include sports like running and swimming. Winter Games are about snow and ice, like skiing and skating. Both happen every four years.

# Olympics — middle (8–11)

<!-- age: middle | lang: en | topic: sports | subtopic: olympics -->

Q: What is the Olympic torch relay?
A: Runners carry a lit torch from Greece toward the host city before the Games begin. The flame stays burning to show peace and friendship among nations.

---

Q: What is a marathon?
A: It is a very long running race, about 42 kilometers (26 miles). The name comes from an ancient Greek story about a messenger who ran a long way to bring news.

---

C: Athletes [warm up] their muscles before competing so they are less likely to get hurt.

---

Q: What happens at an Olympic medal ceremony?
A: The top three athletes stand on a podium. Gold is in the middle on the highest step, silver on one side, and bronze on the other. The gold medalist often hears their country's anthem.

---

# Olympics — middle (8–11)

<!-- age: middle | lang: en | topic: sports | subtopic: olympics -->

Q: What are the Paralympics?
A: They are a huge sports event like the Olympics for athletes with disabilities. Riders, runners, and swimmers compete and try to win medals.

---

Q: What is the Olympic Village?
A: It is a neighborhood of buildings where athletes sleep, eat, and meet during the Games. It helps people from different countries get to know one another.

---

C: Breaking an Olympic [record] means doing something faster, higher, or stronger than anyone has before at the Games.
